      Questions

      Why do drainers use signatures instead of hacking?

      Because it's easier. A signed permit or approval is a legitimate blockchain permission — you granted it yourself. There's nothing to "hack back" and usually nothing to reverse. That's why the defence has to happen in the ten seconds before you sign.

      What should I paste, exactly?

      Whatever your wallet shows you: the typed-data JSON (wallets show it under "message" or "details"), the approval screen's contents, or the message text. The more complete the paste, the more precise the decode. A bare risk word with no spender or amount can't be fully decoded.

      I already signed something bad. What now?

      Act in this order: (1) move any remaining funds to a brand-new wallet, (2) from a clean setup, revoke the old approvals — revoke.cash shows them by wallet address, (3) never sign anything sent to you about "recovering" the loss — recovery scams are the second wave.

      Does SignSafe ever need my wallet connected or my keys?

      Never. No connect button, no keys, no seed phrase — ever. Any tool that asks for those to "check" a signature is the thing you should be checking.
